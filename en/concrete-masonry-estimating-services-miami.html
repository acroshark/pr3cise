Concrete and masonry estimating services in Miami

Foundations, slabs, CMU, formwork and reinforcing measured to the assembly — the structural backbone of almost every South Florida building, and the trade where HVHZ costs the most.

Concrete is measured by pour and by element — footings, slabs, columns, beams, shear walls — with formwork, reinforcement and finishing quantified separately, and masonry counted by unit type with grout and reinforcement broken out. Every project inside Miami-Dade sits in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, which drives reinforcement density, tie-down and connection detailing, and the inspection holds that shape your pour schedule.

What we price on a Miami concrete package

  • Foundations and footings: excavation, forming, reinforcing, concrete by mix design and placement method, with pile caps and grade beams where they apply.
  • Slabs on grade and elevated, including vapor barrier, mesh or rebar, finishing, curing and joint treatment.
  • CMU walls quantified by block size and type, with cells to be filled counted rather than averaged, plus mortar, grout and reinforcing.
  • Tie beams and columns — the poured elements that tie a South Florida masonry building together and carry its wind resistance.
  • Formwork priced by contact area and reuse cycles rather than as a percentage of concrete cost, which is how form-heavy jobs get underbid.
  • Reinforcing steel quantified by bar size and weight, with laps, chairs, ties and accessories included instead of assumed.

What Miami-Dade changes about this trade

  • HVHZ reinforcing density. Wind resistance in Miami-Dade means more filled cells, heavier tie beams and more reinforcing than the same building elsewhere in Florida. This is the single largest HVHZ cost driver on a masonry structure.
  • Oolitic limestone excavation. South Florida rock is harder to dig than the soils most national cost databases assume, and it changes both productivity and equipment selection.
  • High water table. Shallow groundwater across much of Miami-Dade frequently means dewatering for footings and any below-grade work.
  • Concrete durability near the coast. Chloride exposure drives mix design, cover requirements and sometimes corrosion-inhibiting admixtures, none of which are free.
  • Recertification repair work. Structural concrete restoration driven by Miami-Dade recertification and Florida milestone inspections is a large and distinct market, priced very differently from new construction.
