Fire protection estimating services in Miami

Sprinkler, standpipe and fire pump packages priced to NFPA 13 and the Florida Fire Prevention Code, with hazard classification stated rather than assumed.

Bring in a fire sprinkler cost estimator for the package on your desk: head counts come off the drawings by hazard classification, with hydraulic zones, standpipes, fire pump and the underground lead-in each broken out and priced on their own, so you can see exactly where a code requirement is driving the number. It is one trade inside our construction estimating service for Miami-Dade.

Every project inside Miami-Dade sits in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone as well, which sets the bracing and restraint scope the authority having jurisdiction will hold you to, and the corrosion specification for anything exposed near the water.

What a Miami fire sprinkler package covers

  • Sprinkler systems by hazard classification — light, ordinary or extra — with head counts, pipe sizing, hangers and the bracing required by the applicable standard.
  • Standpipe systems including risers, hose valves, fire department connections and the pressure-regulating devices that high-rise work demands.
  • Fire pumps and controllers with jockey pumps, test headers, flow meters and the pump room piping that is routinely underestimated.
  • Underground fire service, backflow assemblies, post-indicator valves and the site work to the point of connection.
  • Alarm interface: flow switches, tamper switches, and the coordination points between the sprinkler contractor and the fire alarm scope.
  • Storage and special hazard systems where they apply — in-rack or high-challenge sprinklers for high-piled storage, clean agent, kitchen suppression and pre-action for data and electrical rooms.
  • Completion scope: firestopping at penetrations, identification, testing and the inspection sequence the authority will require.

Heads, pipe and hazard class: how the takeoff is built

Everything starts with the hazard classification, because it sets design density and head spacing, and therefore the head count, the pipe sizes and the water supply. We propose the classification from the occupancy and the documents and state it on the estimate rather than leaving it implied. From there: heads by count and by type, temperature rating and finish; drops and armovers counted as their own items; pipe by linear foot for each size and material, measured with the risers, not flat; fittings, hangers and restraints by count; valves, switches and the fire department connection by each; underground lead-in by length with its valves and thrust restraint; and the pump, controller and pump-room piping as a discrete package.

You receive an Excel workbook and a PDF organized to CSI Division 21, with material, labor and equipment separated, the pump and underground kept apart from the wet work above, and the marked-up drawings behind every quantity. Assumptions and exclusions are explicit — stamped hydraulic calculations, fire alarm panel and devices where they sit in the electrical scope, and water supply data we have not been given. The format is visible in our sample estimates.

Where fire protection estimates go wrong

  • The hazard class guessed downward. Pricing a storage or manufacturing occupancy as ordinary hazard is the single most expensive assumption in this trade, and it usually surfaces at plan review.
  • Heads counted, drops forgotten. A head is not a line item on its own: the drop, armover, fittings and escutcheon travel with it, and the ceiling type decides which.
  • Pipe measured flat. Risers, offsets around ductwork and the run back to the riser room are real footage on every floor of a high-rise.
  • The pump room underpriced. The pump is a catalogue number; the suction and discharge piping, valves, test header, flow meter and the room itself are where the hours go.
  • Underground left to somebody else. The lead-in, backflow assembly and point of connection have to belong to a scope. When each bidder assumes the other carries it, the low bid is the one that forgot it.
  • Bracing and restraint omitted. Support and restraint of mains is not optional here, and it interacts with the structural requirements on the rest of the building.

Most of those are scope-boundary failures rather than arithmetic, which is the category examined in the risks of an inaccurate construction estimate.

Florida Fire Prevention Code and HVHZ: what changes here

  • The Florida Fire Prevention Code governs, and it adopts NFPA standards with Florida-specific amendments. Pricing straight to national NFPA without the state amendments produces a number that will not pass review.
  • The authority having jurisdiction is local. Your shop drawings are reviewed by the county fire department or by a city fire marshal, and they do not all ask for the same detail. That shows up in resubmittals and in schedule, both of which cost money.
  • High-rise standpipe and pump requirements. Miami residential and commercial towers carry pressure zone, redundancy and fire pump requirements that dominate the cost of the package.
  • Corrosion in coastal and humid conditions. Material and coating selection on exposed and dry-pipe systems is a genuine cost driver in this climate, and near the water it is not a preference.
  • Hurricane-related bracing and attachment. Support and restraint of sprinkler mains interacts with the HVHZ structural requirements on the rest of the building.
  • Retrofit into occupied buildings. Sprinklering older Miami-Dade stock, or upgrading after a change of occupancy, brings phasing, night work and existing-conditions risk that new construction never carries.

Where we price fire protection work in Miami-Dade

The package looks different depending on what is being built. Brickell, Downtown and Aventura towers are standpipe, pressure-zone and fire pump jobs, where the riser and the pump room carry the cost. Doral and Medley distribution warehousing turns on commodity classification and water supply — a storage occupancy priced as ordinary hazard is a different building. Hialeah light industrial and Miami Beach hotel work bring retrofit, change of occupancy and corrosion specification together. Because the fire scope touches the other trades constantly, we price it alongside them when it helps: the electrical estimate carries the fire alarm panel and devices and the pump feeder, while the MEP package resolves the ceiling space where sprinkler mains, duct and conduit all want to be.
