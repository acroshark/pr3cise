Fire protection estimating services in Miami

Sprinkler, standpipe and fire pump packages priced to NFPA 13 and the Florida Fire Prevention Code, with hazard classification stated rather than assumed.

Every project inside Miami-Dade sits in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone. That is not a footnote in the estimate — it is the estimate. HVHZ drives product selection, connection density, inspection sequencing and lead times, and an estimator who prices a Miami job off national averages will be wrong in the same three places every time.

What we price on a Miami fire package

  • Sprinkler systems by hazard classification — light, ordinary or extra — with head counts, pipe sizing, hangers and the bracing required by the applicable standard.
  • Standpipe systems including risers, hose valves, fire department connections and the pressure-regulating devices that high-rise work demands.
  • Fire pumps and controllers with jockey pumps, test headers, flow meters and the pump room piping that is routinely underestimated.
  • Underground fire service, backflow assemblies, post-indicator valves and the site work to the point of connection.
  • Alarm interface: flow switches, tamper switches, and the coordination points between the sprinkler contractor and the fire alarm scope.
  • Special hazard systems where applicable — clean agent, kitchen suppression and pre-action for data and electrical rooms.

What Miami-Dade changes about this trade

  • The Florida Fire Prevention Code governs, and it adopts NFPA standards with Florida-specific amendments. Pricing straight to national NFPA without the state amendments produces a number that will not pass review.
  • High-rise standpipe and pump requirements. Miami residential and commercial towers carry pressure zone, redundancy and fire pump requirements that dominate the cost of the package.
  • Corrosion in coastal and humid conditions. Material and coating selection on exposed and dry-pipe systems is a genuine cost driver in this climate.
  • Hurricane-related bracing and attachment. Support and restraint of sprinkler mains interacts with the HVHZ structural requirements on the rest of the building.
