Electrical estimating services in Miami

Counted, circuited and priced — service through devices, with labor hours you can check against your own crews instead of a lump sum you have to trust.

Hire an electrical cost estimator for the bid you are working on now: your panel schedules, feeder runs and branch circuits come back measured device by device, with conductor and conduit quantified separately so a copper price move can be repriced without disturbing the rest of the estimate. This is one trade inside our construction estimating service for Miami-Dade, and it is the one subcontractors ask for most.

Every project inside Miami-Dade also sits in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, which decides enclosure ratings, exterior equipment anchorage, generator and transfer-switch scope and the standby provisions the inspector will look for — precisely the line items an estimator working off national averages leaves out.

What a Miami electrical contractor gets in the estimate

  • Service and distribution. Service entrance, meter stacks, switchgear, panelboards, transformers, feeders sized and priced by run, grounding and bonding.
  • Branch circuiting counted by home run rather than estimated per square foot, with conduit and conductor priced by type, size and actual routed length.
  • Lighting and controls. Fixture counts against the schedule, drivers, dimming and occupancy controls, emergency and egress lighting, exterior and site lighting.
  • Devices and equipment connections including the mechanical equipment hookups that fall between the electrical and mechanical scopes and get priced by neither.
  • Fire alarm and low-voltage rough-in, data and communications pathways, and the conduit and back-box counts that go with them.
  • Site and support scope. Duct bank, trenching and backfill, temporary power, generator and transfer switch where the occupancy calls for one, plus testing and commissioning.
  • Labor priced by NECA-style productivity rates, shown per line item so the hours are auditable rather than assumed.

How the quantities are measured, and what lands in your inbox

Electrical quantities only mean something in their own units, so that is how they stay. Conduit and conductor by linear foot, separated by material, size and installation method — EMT in a ceiling and PVC in a duct bank are not the same labor hour. Devices, fixtures, gear and equipment connections by each, taken off the schedules rather than the floor plan alone. Feeders by routed length with the vertical runs counted, not by the straight line between two rooms. Excavation for site work by volume, and fittings, supports, hangers and back boxes counted as items rather than swallowed by a percentage.

You get an Excel workbook and a PDF organized to CSI Division 26, with material, labor and equipment split on every line, labor hours visible per item, and the marked-up drawings so any quantity can be traced back to the sheet it came from. Assumptions and exclusions are written out: gear the drawings leave to a design-build vendor, utility charges, work outside the electrical scope, and anything the documents genuinely do not answer. You can see the format in our sample estimates.

Where electrical takeoffs go wrong

  • Square-foot pricing on a hard bid. Fine for a conceptual budget, dangerous on a bid: two buildings of the same area differ enormously in home-run count depending on how the space is chopped up.
  • Feeders measured flat. Riser height, offsets and routing around structure are real copper. Measuring feeders as plan distance is one of the fastest ways to lose money on a high-rise.
  • Equipment connections nobody owns. Rooftop units, pumps, dampers and kitchen equipment sit between the mechanical and electrical scopes and routinely get priced by neither.
  • Lighting taken off a superseded schedule. Fixture types change in addenda more than any other item on an electrical job, so the count is re-run against the current schedule.
  • Empty pathway forgotten. Conduit, sleeves and back boxes for data, security and AV are usually drawn on someone else's sheet and still belong in your number.
  • Small stuff by percentage. Fittings, supports, firestopping and identification are individually trivial and collectively a margin.

Each of those is a quiet undercount rather than a visible mistake, which is exactly what makes them expensive — the pattern is the same one described in the risks of an inaccurate construction estimate.

What Miami-Dade changes about electrical work

  • Wind-load anchorage on exterior equipment. Meter stacks, disconnects, generators and exterior enclosures in the HVHZ need attachment details, and often product approvals, that a national estimate will not carry.
  • Corrosion-resistant materials near the coast. Salt-air exposure drives enclosure ratings and support material on a large share of Miami-Dade projects, and the difference between a standard enclosure and a coastal one is not small.
  • Equipment raised out of the flood plain. On low-lying and coastal sites, gear and transfer equipment have to sit above the design flood elevation, which changes room layout, feeder lengths and sometimes the structure that carries them.
  • Emergency and standby power. Miami-Dade high-rise, assembly and healthcare occupancies routinely carry generator, ATS and emergency-branch requirements that materially change the number.
  • The code that actually applies. The National Electrical Code as adopted through the Florida Building Code, with the HVHZ provisions on top — we price to the edition in force for your permit rather than the national base document.
  • Storm-hardening scopes. Post-hurricane resilience upgrades and recertification-driven electrical remediation are a recurring source of work here, and they price differently from new construction.

Where we price electrical work in Miami-Dade

We price electrical packages across the county, and the estimate changes character with the address. Brickell and Downtown towers are riser, metering and emergency-branch jobs, where feeder length and standby scope dominate. Doral and Medley warehousing turns on service capacity, high-bay lighting and 480-volt distribution. Hialeah's older light-industrial stock brings service upgrades and existing conditions that never appear on a plan. Coral Gables, Pinecrest and Kendall residential leans on site lighting, generators and underground service; Miami Beach, Aventura and Key Biscayne add corrosion ratings and elevated equipment; Wynwood and Little Havana adaptive reuse is demolition, unknowns and a new service. Homestead, Cutler Bay, and Fort Lauderdale and Hollywood across the Broward line, round out the map.

Permitting runs through Miami-Dade County in unincorporated areas and through the municipal building department everywhere else, each with its own fee schedule and inspection habits, and the serving utility is not the same body in every municipality. Electrical rarely travels alone either: if the mechanical and plumbing need pricing with it, take it as an MEP estimating package, and where a sprinkler scope is attached, the fire protection estimate covers the flow and tamper interface.
