Plumbing estimating services in Miami

Fixture counts, pipe measured by size and material, and the underground work that decides whether the job makes money — priced to the Florida Building Code Plumbing volume.

Put a plumbing cost estimator on the package before you commit a price. Underground gets its own broken-out section — excavation, bedding, pipe, backfill and compaction — because on a South Florida slab-on-grade job that is where the margin is won or lost. Above it, fixtures, carriers, water and waste risers and roof drainage are counted individually. This is one trade inside our construction estimating service for Miami-Dade.

And since every project inside Miami-Dade sits in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, roof drainage, rooftop equipment restraint and exposed piping carry requirements a national average never accounts for.

What a Miami plumbing package covers

  • Underground rough-in measured properly: excavation, bedding, pipe, backfill and compaction, which on a South Florida slab-on-grade job is where the margin is won or lost.
  • Above-ground rough-in. Domestic hot and cold water, sanitary waste and vent, storm and roof drainage, priced by size and material with fittings counted rather than percentage-guessed.
  • Fixtures and trim counted against the fixture schedule, with carriers, stops, escutcheons and the accessories that vanish from quick takeoffs.
  • Gas piping sized and priced by run, including regulators, valves and the restraints required where exposed.
  • Water heaters, pumps and specialties including booster and sump systems, expansion tanks, backflow preventers, mixing valves and grease interceptors.
  • Insulation, hangers and supports priced as their own lines, because rolling them into a percentage is how plumbing bids go wrong.
  • Site connection and commissioning. The run to the point of connection, sleeves and firestopping, testing, disinfection and the inspection sequence that goes with them.

Measured by size, material and run — not by fixture count alone

Fixture count is a sanity check, not an estimate. Pipe is measured by linear foot for every size and material separately, because cast iron, copper, PVC and CPVC do not share a labor hour or a hanger spacing. Fittings, valves, cleanouts, floor and roof drains, carriers and specialties are counted as items. Trench work is measured by length and volume, with bedding and compaction carried on their own, and rock is a line rather than a surprise. Insulation goes in by linear foot per pipe size, hangers by count and spacing, and testing and disinfection by system.

You receive an Excel workbook and a PDF organized to CSI Division 22, with material, labor and equipment separated on every line, the underground presented as its own chapter, and marked-up drawings so any quantity traces back to the riser or plan it came from. Assumptions and exclusions are written out: utility connection charges, work in someone else's scope, fixtures supplied by the owner, and anything the drawings leave to a design-build vendor. The format is visible in our sample estimates.

Where plumbing takeoffs lose money

  • Underground folded into a lump. The one item most worth breaking out is the one most often buried, and it is the item that moves most between a clean sand dig and a rock trench.
  • Fittings by percentage. Acceptable at concept stage, expensive on a fitting-dense commercial rough-in where the fitting count can rival the pipe.
  • Carriers and supports forgotten. Wall-hung fixtures need carriers, and hangers, seismic-style supports and sleeves are real items with real labor.
  • Storm sized off a national rainfall table. South Florida design storms drive larger leaders, drains and horizontal runs than most of the country, and an undersized takeoff carries straight through to the bid.
  • Testing and disinfection left out. Hydrostatic testing, chlorination and the re-tests that follow a failed inspection are labor nobody pays you for later.
  • Existing conditions assumed. On a repipe or a build-out, what is actually in the wall is rarely what is on the drawing — that risk belongs in writing, as an assumption or an exclusion.

These are quiet, cumulative losses rather than visible mistakes, and they are exactly the ones costed out in how construction cost estimating saves money.

Water table, code and coast: what Miami-Dade changes

  • High water table and dewatering. Much of Miami-Dade sits on shallow groundwater. Underground plumbing frequently needs dewatering that a national estimating template will not price.
  • Limestone and rock excavation. South Florida oolitic limestone is not the soft dig most cost databases assume. Trenching productivity here is genuinely different, and so is the equipment.
  • Storm drainage sized for South Florida rainfall. Design storm intensities drive larger roof drains, leaders and horizontal storm than in most of the country.
  • Backflow and cross-connection control. Miami-Dade Water and Sewer or the municipal utility sets assembly and testing requirements that belong in the estimate rather than in a change order.
  • HVHZ on the roof and outside. Roof drains, vents, rooftop piping and exposed runs need restraint and attachment that hold in the zone, and exposed metal near the coast gets specified accordingly.
  • Repipe and recertification work. Older Miami-Dade stock generates a steady flow of riser replacement and remediation priced from an inspection report, with occupied-building access and phasing that new construction never faces.

Where we price plumbing work in Miami-Dade

Plumbing changes shape across the county. Brickell, Downtown and Aventura high-rise work is risers, pressure zones and booster pumps, where vertical distribution dominates the number. Miami Beach and Key Biscayne sit low and near salt water, which drives storm capacity, corrosion-resistant specification and equipment raised out of the flood plain. Doral, Medley and Hialeah industrial brings grease interceptors, process piping and large-diameter site runs. Coral Gables, Pinecrest and Kendall residential is fixture-dense and finish-sensitive; Homestead and Cutler Bay tract work is slab-on-grade underground, and in parts of the county still on on-site sewage systems, that belongs in the number too. Wynwood and Little Havana adaptive reuse is mostly repipe and unknowns. Broward work in Fort Lauderdale and Hollywood prices the same way.

Permitting runs through Miami-Dade County in unincorporated areas and each municipal building department elsewhere, and the water and sewer authority is not the same body everywhere either, which affects connection requirements and testing. Where the job runs wider than one trade, the MEP estimating package prices mechanical and electrical alongside it, and the concrete and masonry estimate covers the slab your underground goes under.
