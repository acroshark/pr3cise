What Miami-Dade actually changes in a construction estimate

  • High-Velocity Hurricane Zone compliance. The HVHZ covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties, and it governs impact-rated openings, roof-to-wall connection density, enhanced fastening schedules and the product approvals that go with them. HVHZ is not a line item you add at the end — it changes assemblies throughout the building.
  • Notice of Acceptance constraints. Miami-Dade product control issues a Notice of Acceptance for products used in the HVHZ, each with its own number and expiry date, and the permit submittal has to carry them. A statewide product approval does not automatically satisfy the zone. That removes cheaper unapproved substitutes from the pricing set: we price the approved assembly your specification names rather than guessing a replacement.
  • Wind-borne debris protection. Openings need impact-rated glazing or approved shutters, and the choice between them is one of the largest single swings in a South Florida envelope budget. We price it per opening type, off the window and door schedule.
  • Permit fees priced as real money. Building permit fees are calculated from the declared construction value under the fee schedule of whichever authority has jurisdiction, and each municipality runs its own. It is the most commonly forgotten line in the budgets we are asked to review.
  • Recertification and milestone inspection work. Miami-Dade's building recertification program and Florida's milestone inspection requirements generate a steady stream of repair scopes, priced straight from the engineer's report and itemized so an owner or association sees what each recommendation costs.
  • Local labor and material rates. Miami-Dade wage rates and South Florida material pricing, cross-checked against live supplier quotes, not a national average that understates the job by double digits.
  • Coastal and flood-zone conditions. Elevation requirements, corrosion-resistant fastening, salt-air material selection, and a water table shallow enough across much of the county to put dewatering into the groundworks price.

None of that is exotic knowledge in Miami — your own project manager knows it. The problem is that generic estimating templates do not, which is how a job gets bid at a national average and built at a Miami price. How a Miami estimate gets built, in four steps

The same four steps on every job, one trade or the whole building. What we need from you is listed with each one, because a missing schedule is the usual reason an estimate slips.

Takeoff and scope in

Send your quantity takeoff, plus the bid date and the trades you want priced. Excel is best; PDF is fine. We confirm the fixed fee and the delivery date in writing before anything starts, and we say so plainly if your deadline is not realistic.

02

Takeoff review

We read your takeoff line by line before pricing it. Areas, lengths, counts and volumes stay in your own units rather than being converted to a single lump. Unclear descriptions and scope gaps are logged as we find them, not swallowed.

03

Pricing and assumptions

Material, labor and equipment are priced separately on each line with South Florida rates, waste factors applied per material, and productivity shown in hours. Anything the takeoff does not answer becomes a written assumption or an exclusion, so your risk is visible before bid day rather than after award.

04

Delivery and review

You receive the estimate in Excel and PDF, organized by CSI MasterFormat division. We walk you through it if you want, adjust markups to your numbers, and reprice affected sections when an addendum lands or the scope moves.

What you receive, and what the estimate leaves out

The deliverable is a working preconstruction package prepared by a technical architect, not a one-page summary sheet. It arrives as an unlocked Excel workbook plus a formatted PDF, structured by CSI MasterFormat division, with material, labor and equipment separated on every line, your quantities in their own units, unit rates, per-material waste factors, crew hours, and general conditions, overhead and profit broken out where you can edit them. Every price sits next to the line of your takeoff it belongs to, so any number can be traced in seconds.

The basis of the estimate is stated clearly on the cover. A budget priced from preliminary quantities does not carry the same tolerance as a hard-bid estimate priced from a complete takeoff. We state which takeoff version we priced, and how complete it was, upfront so owners, lenders and GCs know what contingency is appropriate.

Exclusions are written down. Fees the authority has not confirmed, utility connection charges, owner-supplied equipment, escalation beyond the stated validity date, bonds and insurance at rates we do not know, unforeseen ground conditions, and any scope your takeoff leaves to a design-build subcontractor: each is listed explicitly instead of hidden in a lump. When an addendum lands, Pr3cise reprices the affected sections and reissues rather than patching a total. South Florida coverage: Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach

We price commercial, residential and industrial projects across Miami-Dade County, Broward County and Palm Beach County. Because takeoffs and estimates are exchanged digitally, your office location makes zero difference to turnaround.

What does change across South Florida is the authority having jurisdiction and the building typology. Unincorporated Miami-Dade and individual municipalities such as the City of Miami, Miami Beach, Coral Gables, Doral and Hialeah each maintain distinct permit fee schedules. Likewise, an urban high-rise or hospitality fit-out prices very differently from inland warehouse tilt-up construction, while barrier-island and coastal projects carry corrosion-resistant hardware, flood-elevation and dewatering costs that inland projects rarely encounter.
