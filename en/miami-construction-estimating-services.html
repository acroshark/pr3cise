What Miami-Dade actually changes in a construction estimate

  • High-Velocity Hurricane Zone compliance. Impact-rated openings, roof-to-wall connection density, enhanced fastening schedules and the product approvals that go with them. HVHZ is not a line item you add at the end — it changes assemblies throughout the building.
  • Notice of Acceptance constraints. Miami-Dade requires an NOA for products used in the HVHZ, and the permit lists every NOA number in the assembly. That removes cheaper unapproved substitutes from the pricing set, and an estimate that ignores it will not survive permitting.
  • Permit fees priced as real money. Miami-Dade calculates building permit fees as a percentage of construction value. On a commercial job that is a meaningful number, and it is the single most commonly forgotten line in the budgets we are asked to review.
  • Recertification and milestone inspection work. Miami-Dade's building recertification program and Florida's milestone inspection requirements generate a steady stream of repair scopes. We price structural and electrical remediation packages from the inspection report.
  • Local labor and material rates. Miami-Dade wage rates and South Florida material pricing, not a national average that quietly understates the job by double digits.
  • Coastal and flood-zone conditions. Elevation requirements, corrosion-resistant fastening and salt-air material selection where the project sits near the water.
