What a construction takeoff is, and where it stops

A construction takeoff is the measurement stage of a bid: how much of each item the drawings contain, in its proper unit — square metres or square feet, linear metres or feet, cubic metres or cubic yards, tonnes, each. Nothing is priced. The output is a schedule of quantities, not money.

An estimate is that same measurement with cost attached: labour, material, equipment, waste, overhead and profit. The two words get used interchangeably, and it causes arguments: so we say it plainly: the quantity takeoff is yours, and the pricing is what Pr3cise adds.

If you do not have a takeoff yet, get the measurement done first — by your own team, your design team or a takeoff provider — and then send it to us. If you do, the Pr3cise estimating service turns it into a bid-ready number. A priced takeoff has the virtue of being checkable: anyone can pick a line, see the quantity, the unit price and the build-up, and confirm the total — which is exactly what our guide on how to review a contractor’s estimate walks through, line by line.

What gets priced, trade by trade

Every trade has its own units, and its own habitual omissions — the ones that cost money.

Concrete, masonry and rebar

Volumes by mix and pour, formwork as contact area rather than floor area, rebar by bar mark converted to weight, blockwork by unit size.

Structural steel and framing

Members priced by weight, connections and base plates as their own items, stud partitions by length with gauge and height kept apart.

Electrical

Devices and fixtures priced each, branch circuits by conductor size, conduit by diameter and run type, gear and transformers priced as equipment.

Plumbing and piping

Underground priced apart from above-ground: different crews, different risk. Pipe by material and diameter, fittings and valves as items, fixtures against the schedule.

Mechanical and HVAC

Ductwork by sheet weight and gauge, equipment priced by tag, grilles and diffusers by type, hangers and insulation priced as lines rather than allowed for.

Envelope, finishes and sitework

Openings from the door and window schedules, glazing by area and type, roofing by assembly, drywall by face, earthwork by cut and fill.

One trade or the whole building. If the job needs pricing too, the full range of estimating services covers the same trades with rates attached.

Deliverable formats: Excel, PDF and the coding your market tenders in

  • An Excel workbook and a PDF of the same content. One row per line of your takeoff, with your quantity and unit and our unit price and total in their own columns, so you can filter it, pivot it or send a trade out to a supplier.
  • The build-up behind each price. Material, labour and equipment shown separately, with waste and productivity stated. That is what makes a price auditable: pick a line, read how it was built.
  • CSI MasterFormat coding, division by division, so the priced takeoff drops into the structure your bid and accounting software already expects.
  • NRM2 or SMM bills of quantities, priced item by item where the tender calls for one, keeping the descriptions a quantity surveyor expects.
  • Capítulos and partidas (BC3) — each partida of your mediciones priced, with its measurement lines kept as you sent them.
  • A bill of quantities to POMI or NRM2, priced for FIDIC-based contracts, as the documents require.
  • Imperial or metric, as your takeoff is, consistent throughout, plus a written scope and exclusion list: what was priced, what was not, what the takeoff left ambiguous.

If you have your own template, send it — pricing into a form your team already reads beats anything Pr3cise would choose.

How to send us your takeoff

The only thing that genuinely delays pricing is an item nobody can identify, so the first step asks for clear descriptions.

01

Send the set

Email the Excel file or PDF to contact@pr3cise.com, or link to wherever it already lives: a shared drive, a project portal, a transfer link. Excel is fastest, but nothing needs converting. Include specifications, addenda, schedules and your date.

02

Scope, fee and date in writing

We read the set before quoting, then confirm the scope, the fixed fee and the delivery date in writing. If your deadline is not realistic for the size of the set, we say so then, not later.

03

Pricing

Each line of your takeoff is priced in turn: material, labour and equipment at the rates of your market. Areas, lengths, counts and volumes stay in your own units. Gaps and inconsistencies are logged, not assumed away.

04

Delivery and one revision

You get the workbook and the PDF, with the assumptions and the question list. One revision round is included: an addendum, a scope change, a section repriced.

What happens when the takeoff is incomplete

Almost every takeoff is incomplete somewhere. A unit is missing, a description stops at a product family and leaves the selection open, a trade is measured in one section and forgotten in another, or a quantity is carried as a lump. None of that stops the pricing.

We price everything the takeoff genuinely answers and split the rest into two lists. Written assumptions, each attached to the lines it affects so it is easy to overturn: tell us the partition is rated rather than standard, and those lines are repriced. And questions for whoever measured, written so you can forward them as an RFI.

What we never do is change or invent a quantity quietly and let it sit in the workbook looking like yours. That is the most expensive habit in this trade: it hides risk exactly where a bid review would find it. We set out how it unravels in the risks of an inaccurate construction estimate.

Turnaround, and what a time zone actually buys you

A priced takeoff comes back in 24 to 72 hours. Which end depends on the takeoff, not on what you are willing to pay: a single trade with clean descriptions sits at the fast end, a full multi-trade package with open questions at the slow one. The date and the fixed fee are agreed before work starts — no hourly billing.

The time zone works for you. Our day starts seven hours ahead of US Eastern Time for most of the year (six hours during seasonal DST transitions) and an hour ahead of Central European Time, so a takeoff sent at the end of a Florida afternoon is priced overnight; for clients in Europe and further east our working day overlaps yours, and a question raised in the morning is answered the same afternoon. Which is why it is worth saying if the tender closes on Friday — we would rather decline than hand back a rushed estimate.
