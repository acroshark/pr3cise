What a construction takeoff is, and where it stops

A construction takeoff is the measurement stage of a bid: how much of each item the drawings contain, in its proper unit — square metres or square feet, linear metres or feet, cubic metres or cubic yards, tonnes, each. Nothing is priced. The output is a schedule of quantities, not money.

An estimate is that same measurement with cost attached: labour, material, equipment, waste, overhead and profit. The two words get used interchangeably, and it causes arguments: a contractor who asked for a quantity takeoff and received a priced document has been handed somebody else's rates for his own labour force.

Buy the takeoff alone when you already hold good rates, when your subcontractors price the work and only need to know how much there is, or when you are auditing somebody else's number. Buy the priced version when you want the whole bid built — that is what the Pr3cise estimating and quantity surveying service does. A takeoff has the virtue of being checkable: anyone can open the marked-up sheet, find the shape and confirm the number — which is exactly what our guide on how to review a contractor’s estimate walks through, line by line.

What gets measured, trade by trade

Every trade has its own units, and its own habitual omissions — the ones that cost money.

Concrete, masonry and rebar

Volumes by mix and pour, formwork as contact area rather than floor area, rebar by bar mark converted to weight, blockwork by unit size.

Structural steel and framing

Members scheduled individually and totalled by weight, connections and base plates counted, stud partitions by length with gauge and height kept apart.

Electrical

Devices and fixtures counted off the schedules, branch circuits by conductor size, conduit by diameter and run type, gear and transformers listed as equipment.

Plumbing and piping

Underground measured apart from above-ground: different crews, different risk. Pipe by material and diameter, fittings and valves counted, fixtures from the schedule.

Mechanical and HVAC

Ductwork by surface area converted to sheet weight by gauge, equipment counted by tag, grilles and diffusers by type, hangers and insulation measured not allowed for.

Envelope, finishes and sitework

Openings from the door and window schedules, glazing by area and type, roofing by assembly, drywall by face, earthwork by cut and fill.

One trade or the whole building. If the job needs pricing too, the full range of estimating services covers the same trades with rates attached.

Deliverable formats: Excel, PDF and the coding your market tenders in

  • An Excel workbook and a PDF of the same content. One row per measured item, quantity, unit and sheet reference in their own columns, so you can filter it, pivot it or send a trade out to a supplier.
  • The marked-up drawings. Every measured shape colour-coded on the sheet it came from. That is what makes a quantity auditable: pick a line, find the shape.
  • CSI MasterFormat coding for North American work, division by division, so the takeoff drops into the structure your bid and accounting software already expects.
  • NRM2 or SMM measurement for United Kingdom tenders, written up as a bill of quantities with the descriptions a quantity surveyor expects — or NRM1 at cost-plan stage.
  • Mediciones in the Spanish convention — capítulos and partidas, each partida carrying its measurement lines with the uds × long × anch × alt breakdown.
  • A bill of quantities for Gulf tenders, measured to POMI or NRM2 as the documents require — what most FIDIC-based contracts in the United Arab Emirates run on.
  • Imperial or metric, your choice, consistent throughout, plus a written scope and exclusion list: what was measured, what was not, what the drawings left ambiguous.

If you have your own template, send it — fitting a takeoff into a form your team already reads beats anything Pr3cise would choose.

How to send us the drawings

The only thing that genuinely delays a takeoff is a missing schedule, so the first step asks for them by name.

01

Send the set

Email the PDFs to contact@pr3cise.com, or link to wherever they already live: a shared drive, a project portal, a transfer link. CAD and IFC are faster to measure, but nothing needs converting. Include specifications, addenda, schedules and your date.

02

Scope, fee and date in writing

We read the set before quoting, then confirm the scope, the fixed fee and the delivery date in writing. If your deadline is not realistic for the size of the set, we say so then, not later.

03

Measurement

Quantities come off the drawings on screen, sheet by sheet, each item colour-marked as it is taken. Areas, lengths, counts and volumes stay in their own units. Conflicts between sheets are logged, not assumed away.

04

Delivery and one revision

You get the workbook, the PDF and the marked-up drawings, with the assumptions and the question list. One revision round is included: an addendum, a scope change, a section re-measured.

What happens when the drawings are incomplete

Almost every set is incomplete somewhere. A schedule is missing, a dimension string stops halfway, the mechanical sheets show equipment the architectural sheets have no room for, or the specification names a product family and leaves the selection to the contractor. None of that stops a takeoff.

We measure everything the drawings genuinely answer and split the rest into two lists. Written assumptions, each attached to the items it affects so it is easy to overturn: tell us the partitions run to the slab rather than the ceiling grid, and those quantities are re-measured. And questions for the design team, written so you can forward them as an RFI.

What we never do is guess a quantity quietly and let it sit in the workbook looking measured. That is the most expensive habit in this trade: it hides risk exactly where a bid review would find it. We set out how it unravels in the risks of an inaccurate construction estimate.

Turnaround, and what a time zone actually buys you

A takeoff comes back in 24 to 72 hours. Which end depends on the set, not on what you are willing to pay: a single trade on a clean, fully scheduled set sits at the fast end, a full multi-trade package with coordination problems at the slow one. The date and the fixed fee are agreed before work starts — no hourly billing.

The time zone works for you. Our day starts seven hours before the United States East Coast and an hour before Madrid, so a set sent at the end of a North American afternoon is measured overnight; for clients in the Gulf our working day overlaps yours, and a question raised in the morning is answered the same afternoon. Which is why it is worth saying if the tender closes on Friday — we would rather decline than hand back a rushed measurement.
